RoFE: Rotated Frozen Embeddings for Low-Data Multimodal Tabular Learning

Yihao Qiao, Jiajian Ma, Wushuang Rui, Abhishek Jha, Aarti Darji, Yiqiu Shen
New York University
Paper Code

Three approaches to incorporating embeddings into PFNs: (a) raw embeddings exceed the 100-feature maximum of TabICLv2 pretraining, (b) the PCA interface collapses the geometry of labeled examples after standardization, and (c) RoFE rotates PCA scores so that standardization approximately preserves the original geometry and class separation.

Abstract

Multimodal tabular learning combines tabular data with images and text, often in settings where labeled data are scarce. Tabular foundation models such as prior-data fitted networks (PFNs) offer a promising solution via in-context learning without task-specific training. However, modern image and text encoders produce embeddings with hundreds or thousands of dimensions, far exceeding the number of features encountered during PFN pretraining. Existing approaches bridge this gap using principal component analysis (PCA) to compress frozen embeddings. Surprisingly, we find that this PCA interface can underperform simple linear classifiers when labeled examples are scarce, even though PFNs are designed for small datasets. We identify the underlying failure mechanism: the PFN's per-column standardization removes the variances of the PCA scores. When the PCA keeps every direction of the labeled examples, the examples collapse into a regular simplex in which all pairwise distances are equal. To address this problem, we propose Rotated Frozen Embeddings (RoFE), a training-free method that rotates PCA scores before PFN inference. We theoretically show that rotation makes the column variances nearly equal, so that standardization approximately preserves the embedding geometry. Across image and text classification tasks, encoders, and tabular foundation models, RoFE improves low-data performance over the PCA interface and outperforms baselines that require task-specific training. The improvements also extend to regression.

Collapse of the Labeled Examples

CoMET passes the principal component scores of frozen embeddings to a prior-data fitted network (PFN), which standardizes each column with the mean and the standard deviation of the labeled examples. When the PCA keeps all $n-1$ directions, this removes the variances of the scores and makes the labeled examples a regular simplex, in which every pair of examples is equally far apart, whatever their classes.

Random Rotations

RoFE needs no training: it multiplies the scores of every example, labeled or query, by a random rotation $R$, runs the PFN once for each of $K=4$ rotations and averages the predicted probabilities. The column variances then become nearly equal, so the standardization divides every column by about one common factor and keeps the distances between examples.

Main Results

From 10 to 100 labels per class, RoFE outperforms CoMET with every image and text encoder, with the embedding alone and with the table. Without any training, it also outperforms MultiModalPFN and CatBoost, two baselines that require task-specific training.

Gain by Number of Labeled Examples

With $n$ labeled examples, the gain is largest while $n\le257$, where the PCA keeps all $n-1$ directions and the labeled examples collapse, and it is within a point of zero for $n>1000$, where the PCA keeps 256 of them.

Dense Rotation and Standardization

Rotations only within the strong directions (large variance) or only within the weak ones (small variance) recover part of the gain at most, while a deterministic Hadamard transform, which mixes all of them, does as well as a random rotation. Keeping the variances in the column scales, by one global scale in place of the per-column standardization, gains little, and at the same scale rotated axes outperform the principal axes by +1.5 to +2.2 points with the image alone.
